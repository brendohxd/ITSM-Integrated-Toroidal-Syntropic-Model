\begin{abstract}
Compact spatial topology can source anisotropic vacuum stress.
For a free massless scalar on a rectangular flat three-torus we test an
already-renormalized periodic lattice formula for Casimir energy density and
directional pressures against cutoff convergence, cubic isotropy,
thermodynamic pressure derivatives, conformal trace, dimensional scaling, and
an independent heat-kernel/Mellin evaluator at four cubic and noncubic points.
We then transport the static shape-dependent stress through a declared
instantaneous constitutive closure with \(a^{-4}\) dilution, first as a
perturbation of de~Sitter expansion and then at finite amplitude on a biaxial
Bianchi-I expanding branch with positive cosmological-background density.
The latter reconstructs the mean expansion from the Hamiltonian constraint;
it is not a fixed-de~Sitter or uniformly perturbative calculation.
In the bounded scan reported here,
this closure produces only \emph{transient} passages near the historically
proposed ratio \(\Ht/\Hp=13/12\), with no quasi-plateau or late-time
attractor.  Within the registered domain, this excludes a maintained ratio
for the tested closure and makes the target a discriminator between
instantaneous free-field stress and any future driven or memory-bearing
constitutive law.  It is not a general no-go theorem for a quantum field
evolved and renormalized on a dynamical compact spacetime; galactic-scale
matching, Hubble inference, and a complete compact cosmology are separate
calculations. The registered sensitivity suite remains \texttt{FAIL}
(18/19); qualitative classifications persist in the re-tested cases, but
threshold amplitudes are not certified precision predictions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Compact flat three-manifolds admit mode spectra that generically produce
direction-dependent vacuum expectation values of the stress tensor
\cite{birrell_davies_1982,lachieze_luminet_1995,edery2006}.
That fact is distinct from any claim that a fixed numerical ratio of
directional Hubble rates is a free parameter-free prediction of topology.

For definiteness, we use \(\Ht/\Hp=13/12\) solely as a historical null target
for the closure test.  Neither a cycle-counting premise nor an observational
Hubble value is inserted into the equations.  We isolate two controlled
questions that can be addressed with the declared lattice formula and
biaxial closure integrator (gate package CBR-001):

\begin{enumerate}
\item Does the implemented periodic-scalar stress pass its internal numerical
and thermodynamic controls and depend on the shape moduli?
\item When that static stress is transported by the declared instantaneous
closure on biaxial expansion, does
\(\Ht/\Hp=13/12\) appear as a free-field attractor, a quasi-plateau, or only
as a transient crossing (if at all)?
\end{enumerate}

The answers are: the implemented nonzero-mode static stress passes the internal
identity checks, agrees with an independent evaluator at relative order
\(10^{-6}\), and is shape dependent; and the bounded closure calculation yields only transient
crossings of \(13/12\), with every valid threshold candidate approaching
isotropy through the final integration time \(N=20\).

Shape-dependent Casimir stress itself is not new: rectangular-cavity and
periodic mode sums are established \cite{edery2006}. Negro et al.
\cite{negro2026} derive topology-dependent scalar stress on de~Sitter,
discuss the state-dependent isotropic zero mode, and calculate
Casimir-sourced anisotropic backreaction. The present contribution is narrower:
a reproducible static implementation with local cross-method controls and a
finite-domain target-persistence audit of an explicitly declared
instantaneous closure. It is neither an independent discovery of anisotropic
Casimir stress nor a replacement for their curved-spacetime state calculation.

Companion work on present-epoch scale matching and weak-field matching
invariants \cite{boyd_p1} records independent no-go results for a different
geometric construction.  The Casimir calculation below is logically
independent of that construction.

\section{Rectangular lattice Casimir stress}
\label{sec:lattice}

\subsection{Mode sum and pressures}

For a massless scalar with periodic lengths \((L_1,L_2,L_3)\), CBR-001 takes
as input the already-renormalized rectangular-periodic Epstein-lattice
expression developed in Ref.~\cite{edery2006}:
\begin{equation}
 \rho_{\mathrm{Cas}}=-\frac{\hbar c}{2\pi^2}
 \sum_{\mathbf n\in\mathbb Z^3\setminus\{0\}}
 \frac{1}{\bigl[(n_1L_1)^2+(n_2L_2)^2+(n_3L_3)^2\bigr]^2}.
 \label{eq:casimir-lattice}
\end{equation}
Directional pressures follow thermodynamically,
\begin{equation}
 p_i=-\frac{1}{L_jL_k}\frac{\partial E_{\mathrm{Cas}}}{\partial L_i},
 \qquad E_{\mathrm{Cas}}=L_1L_2L_3\rho_{\mathrm{Cas}}.
 \label{eq:casimir-pressure}
\end{equation}
In code and tables we report dimensionless lattice units with the overall
\(\hbar c\) factor restored only when matching cosmological amplitudes.
Figure~\ref{fig:rect} recalls the rectangular fundamental domain (not an
embedded doughnut \(\mathrm{T}^2\)).

\subsection{Stage-1 validation (cubic point)}

extrapolation order gives \(\rho=-0.837535893\), while an independent
heat-kernel/Mellin evaluation gives \(\rho=-0.837536911\).  Their relative
discrepancy is \(1.22\times10^{-6}\).  We therefore quote
\begin{equation}
 \rho=-0.837537,\qquad
 p_i=\rho/3=-0.279179
\end{equation}

isotropy at machine precision (Table~\ref{tab:stage1}).
The Stage-1 package reports \texttt{STATUS: PASS}.

\subsection{Stage-2 biaxial shape scan}

Let \(r=L_t/L_p\).  CBR-001 Stage~2 holds \(L_p=1\) while varying
\(L_t=rL_p\); it does not hold the mean scale fixed.  Stage~3 converts this
raw table to fixed mean scale using the homogeneous \(r^{8/3}\) stress
rescaling before constructing its source.
The stress \(\rho(r)\), \(p_p(r)\), \(p_t(r)\) and the anisotropy
\(\Delta p\equiv p_t-p_p\) are smooth functions of shape; \(\Delta p\)
changes sign across the cube \(r=1\) (Fig.~\ref{fig:stage2}).
Representative points (lattice units) include
\begin{align}
 r&=1: & \rho&=-0.8375,& \Delta p&\simeq 0,\\
 r&\simeq 1.26: & \rho&=-0.4702,& \Delta p&\simeq +0.247,
\end{align}
where the second line is in the raw fixed-\(L_p\) Stage-2 convention.  Compact
\emph{shape-dependent} anisotropic stress is therefore a concrete control of
the implemented static formula.
It is \emph{not} a universal cycle-counting coefficient independent of the
mode sum.

\section{Biaxial instantaneous-stress closure}
\label{sec:backreaction}

\subsection{Geometry and equations}

For the biaxial Bianchi-I line element
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{d}s^2=-\mathrm{d}t^2+a_p^2\,\mathrm{d}x^2+a_t^2(\mathrm{d}y^2+\mathrm{d}z^2),
\end{equation}
define
\begin{equation}
 H=\frac{\Hp+2\Ht}{3},\qquad
 \delta=\Ht-\Hp,\qquad
 r=\frac{a_t}{a_p}.
\end{equation}
The shear system is the biaxial specialization of Bianchi-I
\cite{ellismaccallum1969}, with \(\kappa=8\pi G\),
\begin{equation}
 \dot\delta+3H\delta=\kappa(p_t-p_p),\qquad
 \dot r=r\delta,
 \label{eq:shear-system}
\end{equation}
and Hamiltonian constraint
\begin{equation}
 3H^2-\frac{\delta^2}{3}=\kappa\rho_{\mathrm{total}}.
 \label{eq:hamiltonian-biaxial}
\end{equation}
Stage~3A treats the verified Casimir tensor as a small radiation-like
perturbation (\(\propto a^{-4}\)) on a positive de~Sitter background and
integrates in \(N=\ln a\).
It does not evolve quantum-field mode functions on the time-dependent
Bianchi-I geometry or renormalize \(\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle\) there.
The dimensionless source-scale ratio \(\epsilon\) absorbs
\(\hbar c/L_\star^4\) relative to the de~Sitter density scale (CBR-001
Stage~3A convention).  No present ITSM derivation fixes this ratio.
It checks the perturbative limit and recorded constraint/continuity residuals
but does \emph{not} by itself establish any preferred Hubble ratio.

Stage~3B instead retains finite source amplitude and evolving shape. It
integrates \((\ln r,\delta)\), using the positive branch
\begin{equation}
 H=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa\rho_{\mathrm{total}}+\delta^2/3}{3}},\qquad
 \rho_{\mathrm{total}}=\rho_{\mathrm{BG}}+\epsilon\rho_{\mathrm{Cas}},
 \label{eq:stage3b-H}
\end{equation}
with constant \(\rho_{\mathrm{BG}}>0\). Thus the mean \(H\) responds to
the source and shear, rather than being fixed at its de~Sitter value. Runs
terminate on loss of the supported shape interval, positive total density,
real expanding branch, or positive directional expansion. A small residual
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:hamiltonian-biaxial}, when \(H\) is reconstructed from that
same equation, checks algebraic consistency, not an independent mean Einstein
equation. A separately evolved Raychaudhuri check has not been demonstrated
by the recorded controls. This is a constitutive-closure test, not full
semiclassical Einstein evolution.

\subsection{Analytic persistence condition}

Let
\begin{equation}
 x=\frac{\delta}{H},\qquad
 q=\frac{\Ht}{\Hp}=\frac{1+x/3}{1-2x/3}.
 \label{eq:q-x}
\end{equation}
The historical target \(q=13/12\) corresponds to \(x_\star=3/38\).
Because \(\mathrm{d}\ln r/\mathrm{d}N=x\), a constant \(q\neq 1\) entails
\(r(N)=r(N_0)\exp[x(N-N_0)]\). It cannot remain in a compact shape interval
\(0<r_{\min}\le r\le r_{\max}<\infty\) for arbitrarily large \(N\).
This is a kinematic obstruction to a persistent nonunit ratio with bounded
shape, independent of a stress prescription; it is not a fixed-torus property.
For a prescribed \(x(N)\), Eq.~\eqref{eq:shear-system} requires the
anisotropic pressure
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_{\mathrm{req}}=\frac{H^2}{\kappa}
 \Bigl[\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}N}
 +x\bigl(3+\tfrac{\mathrm{d}\ln H}{\mathrm{d}N}\bigr)\Bigr].
 \label{eq:required-stress}
\end{equation}
In de~Sitter at fixed \(x=3/38\), this becomes
\(\Pi_{\mathrm{req}}=9H^2/(38\kappa)\).  By contrast, the free Casimir
anisotropy in the declared closure scales as
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_{\mathrm{Cas}}\propto a^{-4}\,r^{8/3}
 \bigl[\widehat p_t(r)-\widehat p_p(r)\bigr].
 \label{eq:casimir-stress-scaling}
\end{equation}
For unbounded expansion \(a\to\infty\) and shape in the compact interval
above, the bounded shape factor makes \(\Pi_{\mathrm{Cas}}\to0\).
If also \(H\to H_\infty>0\),
\(\mathrm{d}\ln H/\mathrm{d}N\to0\), \(x\to x_\star\), and
\(\mathrm{d}x/\mathrm{d}N\to0\), the required stress tends to
\(9H_\infty^2/(38\kappa)\ne0\). These stated asymptotics give a second,
stress-scaling inconsistency; positive \(H\) alone is insufficient.
Neither argument covers unbounded shape, a finite total expansion, or an
\(H\to0\) branch. Nor do they replace dynamical quantum-state evolution or
renormalization. The finite-domain scan below measures transient crossings
and quasi-plateaus through \(N=20\); it is not a numerical proof of an
infinite-time limit or an all-action no-go theorem.

\subsection{The finite-domain Stage-3B ratio test}

The small-source fixed-background profile
\(x(N)=S(e^{-3N}-e^{-4N})\) peaks at \(N=\ln(4/3)\) with maximum
\((27/256)S\); merely reaching \(x_\star\) already requires
\(S\ge 128/171\simeq 0.7485\), outside the strict small-perturbation regime.

A nonlinear root search over initial shapes
\(r_0\in\{1.01,1.05,1.1,1.25,1.5,2,3,4\}\) and source-scale ratios spanning seven decades
finds target-reaching thresholds for five of eight shapes.
Classification vocabulary:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{NO\_CROSSING}: peak \(q\) stays below \(13/12\);
\item \texttt{TRANSIENT\_CROSSING}: touch or brief crossing;
\item \texttt{QUASI\_PLATEAU}: dwell \(\ge 1\) e-fold within \(1\%\) without
attractor criteria;
\item \texttt{ATTRACTOR}: dwell \(\ge 5\) e-folds within \(1\%\), nearby
trajectories converge, and no subsequent decay toward \(q=1\).
\end{itemize}
Results: zero attractors, zero quasi-plateaus, five transient crossings,
two no-crossings, and one trajectory initialized at the upper interpolation
boundary whose outward drive leaves the supported domain
(Table~\ref{tab:stage3b}; Fig.~\ref{fig:ratio}).
Dwell times within \(1\%\) of the target are only
\(\sim 0.27\)--\(0.30\) e-folds.
Every valid threshold candidate approaches \(q=1\) through \(N=20\), consistent with the
declared \(a^{-4}\) source becoming negligible.  The tested nearby
trajectories also decay toward isotropy; none satisfy the registered attractor
criteria.

The source fraction
\(F_{\mathrm{Cas}}=\max_N|\epsilon\rho_{\mathrm{Cas}}/
\rho_{\mathrm{total}}|\) is respectively
\(2.536,\ 0.632,\ 0.273,\ 0.133,\ 0.082\) for
\(r_0=1.1,1.25,1.5,2,3\) at the recorded thresholds. The denominator
includes the negative Casimir density, so the fraction can exceed unity.
Four cases exceed the registered nonperturbative threshold \(F_{\mathrm{Cas}}
\ge0.1\); the last is marginal (\(0.01\le F_{\mathrm{Cas}}<0.1\)).
These are finite-amplitude diagnostics of the declared closure, not
controlled small-source or renormalized quantum-backreaction predictions.

\section{Scope and open routes}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item Free massless scalar only; interacting or multi-field sectors untested.
\item The renormalized static lattice expression is supplied as input.  A local
independent heat-kernel/Mellin evaluation now agrees at four points, but an
external replication and a full-domain error analysis remain outstanding.
\item The time-dependent calculation is an instantaneous \(a^{-4}\) closure,
not a mode-function evolution or curved-spacetime renormalization of
\(\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle\).  Particle production, curvature counterterms,
trace-anomaly and memory terms are not calculated.
\item Rectangular moduli; twisted flat spaces (\(E_2\), \(E_3\)) not scanned.
\item Stage~3A is perturbative on de~Sitter; Stage~3B permits finite amplitude
and constraint-reconstructed \(H\). Four target-reaching thresholds are
nonperturbative and one marginal. No controlled quantum-state approximation
has been established for those finite-amplitude histories.
\item The preregistered sensitivity suite is complete but not all-pass: one
threshold-amplitude stability check missed its \(10^{-8}\) bound, although all
re-tested classifications and the bounded no-attractor conclusion were unchanged.
\item A derived driven or memory stress (CBR-002) is one possible extension,
not a demonstrated solution or an exhaustive classification of other actions.
\item CMB compatibility of any particular \((L_i)\) is not established here.
\end{itemize}

These assumptions delimit the result.  Driven constitutive stress, alternate
topologies, richer field content, and dynamical renormalization remain open
routes that require their own conservation, stability, and observational
tests.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:concl}

A free massless scalar on rectangular flat \(\Tthree\) yields an internally
checked and independently benchmarked nonzero-mode, shape-dependent anisotropic
Casimir stress.
When that static stress is transported as an instantaneous \(a^{-4}\) source
in the finite-amplitude biaxial closure, the ratio \(\Ht/\Hp=13/12\) appears at most as
a brief transient at large source-scale ratios, never as a quasi-plateau or
attractor in the bounded search reported here.  The tested closure therefore
cannot sustain the historically proposed anisotropic expansion or justify a
map to
\(H_t=72.97\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}\).

The bounded negative result has a constructive consequence: it separates a
verified static, shape-dependent stress from the additional constitutive
physics needed to maintain anisotropic expansion. A persistent nonunit ratio
cannot stay in a compact shape interval over unbounded expansion; the
additional stress-scaling obstruction assumes \(H\to H_\infty>0\) and the
regular asymptotics stated in Sec.~\ref{sec:backreaction}. The numerical
negative result concerns only the registered finite-domain search. It does
not exclude unbounded-shape or \(H\to0\) branches, nor a derived
driven, nonlocal, interacting, or multi-field response.  The next test,
CBR-002, must derive such a stress independently, verify conservation and
stability, and only then compare its evolution with the target ratio without
inserting that ratio into a coefficient.
