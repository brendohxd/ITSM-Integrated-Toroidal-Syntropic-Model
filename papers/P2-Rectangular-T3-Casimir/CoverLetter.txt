Dear Editor / arXiv moderators,

Please find attached a technical paper on an implemented periodic-scalar
Casimir stress for a rectangular flat three-torus and an instantaneous
biaxial-closure control using that static stress.

The manuscript has a deliberately limited claim set:

  (1) We test an already-renormalized lattice Casimir expression for energy
      density and directional pressures against cutoff convergence, cubic
      isotropy, thermodynamic derivatives, conformal trace and dimensional
      scaling. An independent noncubic zeta/Ewald audit remains a stated
      limitation.

  (2) Transporting that static stress through a declared instantaneous a^-4
      closure on a positive de Sitter biaxial Bianchi-I background, we search
      for the historically proposed ratio Ht/Hp = 13/12. In the bounded scan
      the ratio appears at most as a brief transient; we find no quasi-plateau
      and no attractor. Every valid candidate returns to isotropy by late
      expansion within this closure.

We do not claim a resolution of the Hubble tension, a parameter-free prediction
H0 = 72.97 km/s/Mpc, a derivation of a galactic acceleration scale, or a
completed cubic cosmology compatible with Planck topology bounds. Persistent
anisotropy, if present in a fuller theory, requires a derived driven or memory
stress beyond free Casimir (open programme, not this paper).

The manuscript does not claim to evolve and renormalize quantum-field modes
on a dynamical compact Bianchi-I spacetime.

Public code and stage summaries are in the companion repository under
Analysis/Casimir/CBR-001/.
