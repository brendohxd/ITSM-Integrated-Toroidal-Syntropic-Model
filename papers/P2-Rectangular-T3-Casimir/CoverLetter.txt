Dear Editor / arXiv moderators,

Please find attached a technical paper on an implemented periodic-scalar
Casimir stress for a rectangular flat three-torus and an instantaneous
biaxial-closure control using that static stress.

The manuscript has a deliberately limited claim set:

  (1) We test an already-renormalized lattice Casimir expression for energy
      density and directional pressures against cutoff convergence, cubic
      isotropy, thermodynamic derivatives, conformal trace and dimensional
      scaling. A separate heat-kernel/Mellin implementation agrees at four
      cubic and noncubic points at relative order 10^-6. A preregistered
      sensitivity suite preserves every qualitative classification, although
      one threshold-amplitude comparison missed its deliberately stringent
      10^-8 bound (18/19 checks overall).

  (2) Transporting that static stress through a declared instantaneous a^-4
      closure, first perturbatively on de Sitter (Stage 3A), then at finite
      amplitude with constraint-reconstructed biaxial expansion (Stage 3B), we search
      for the historically proposed ratio Ht/Hp = 13/12. In the bounded scan
      the ratio appears at most as a brief transient; we find no quasi-plateau
      and no attractor. Every valid threshold candidate approaches isotropy
      through N = 20. Four threshold cases are nonperturbative and one marginal;
      they are not controlled quantum-backreaction predictions.

Before the finite-domain scan, the manuscript derives the anisotropic stress
required by a persistent ratio. Kinematically, a constant nonunit ratio cannot
remain in a compact shape interval over unbounded expansion. The additional
a^-4 stress-decay argument assumes H tends to a nonzero constant with the
regular asymptotics stated in the paper; positive H alone is insufficient. The
numerical scan then quantifies the resulting transient crossings and late-time
return toward isotropy.

The conclusion is restricted to that closure. Galactic-scale matching, Hubble
inference, Planck topology compatibility, and a quantum stress tensor evolved
and renormalized on dynamical compact Bianchi-I spacetime are separate
calculations. A persistent state in a fuller theory would require an
independently derived constitutive physics outside the tested assumptions;
driven or memory-bearing stress is one open route (CBR-002), not the only
possible class of extensions. The paper positions this target-persistence
audit against established static results and Negro et al.'s state-based work.
