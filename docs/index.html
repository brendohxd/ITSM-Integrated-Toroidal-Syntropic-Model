Integrated Toroidal–Syntropic Model

An active vacuum.
A testable cosmology.

What if the vacuum has a physical structure that matters for gravity? ITSM explores how a finite-density condensate, compact space and exchanges of energy with matter could connect phenomena across cosmic scales.

Explore the idea See the progress

A developing research framework—not yet an observationally validated alternative to standard cosmology.

The central question

Can one physical framework connect the small and the cosmic?

The proposal brings together vacuum excitations, the global structure of space and open-system thermodynamics.

The aim is to derive their consequences, then test the same physics against galaxies, lensing and cosmic expansion.

What ITSM means

Three ideas. One programme of physical tests.

01 · Integrated

A shared physical explanation

Explore whether vacuum, matter and gravity can be described together, with compatible predictions at different scales—not a separate adjustment for each observation.

02 · Toroidal

Space with periodic connections

The proposed spatial topology is a three-torus, T³: a periodic connection in each of three spatial directions. It is not a claim that the universe is a doughnut embedded in space.

03 · Syntropic

Order through physical exchange

Investigate how a subsystem can develop structure while exchanging energy and entropy with its environment. This does not mean reversing time or abandoning conservation for the combined system.

The vision

Connect the phenomena.
Earn the predictions.

The long-term aim is a coherent account of galaxy dynamics, gravitational lensing and cosmic evolution, grounded in the proposed vacuum physics.

A successful model must also recover the gravity we already test locally, remain stable and make predictions that observations can reject. These are research objectives, not completed achievements.

Progress, without the jargon

Research checkpoint: 21 October 2026

Completed in a limited scope

A controlled dust calculation

The registered short-time linear calculation now has explicit correction terms and certified error bounds. It is a mathematical milestone—not yet proof of a physically viable cosmology.

New · first-order result

Following a changing background

For a declared class of small disturbances, a changing-background term cancels an earlier frozen-background forcing at first order. This is a useful compatibility result—not yet a solved evolution or proof of stability.

Not yet predicted

Matter coupling and acceleration

The physical validity window, healthy recovery of general relativity and independent matter matching remain open. No numerical acceleration scale has yet been fixed independently of target data.
