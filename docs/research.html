Condensate state and history · 19 November 2026

Keeping the information that drives the response

A smaller set of field values can look identical while omitted condensate motion changes what happens next. In the registered conditional R4C1/B1 calculation, that motion carries pressure-rate information. A description that retains its history now reproduces the full calculated response across all five tested modes, with controlled numerical errors.

The history evolution was advanced without supplying the full-system trajectory to the solver. This completes a computational consistency test within the existing conditional equations. Physical stability, a viable cosmology and agreement with observations remain separate questions.

Scope and numerical description

The calculation advances eight retained quantities and four history integrals for the registered radial-rate perturbation, through t = 0.05 in the benchmark's time units. All five modes and the high-mode sign reversal are retained. The history integrals preserve information; the result does not establish a new fundamental force, thermodynamic bath or general short-memory approximation.

State/history result, errors and limits

Separate comparison action · R-tau

From initial control toward reliable evolution

R-tau remains an unadopted comparison action. New calculations extend its coordinate and current-constraint work beyond the initial conformal metric and bound how the current operator changes at the initial instant. The earlier calculation keeps all twenty perturbation directions, including the zero-speed and fast-force pairs.

A nonlinear test found that the existing momentum bound alone cannot control a squared-momentum density term. Stronger conditional energy estimates and an exact curvature-trace identity now make the next calculation more concrete. This limitation of the estimate does not demonstrate physical instability.

The remaining test: derive the general-planar metric, frame and matter system and both coupling blocks from the full action, then control the complete evolution and changing constraints on a common domain. The new force-sector identities do not yet identify that final constrained system. A physical interaction window, healthy recovery of general relativity and independent matter/force matching remain open.

A separate observational comparator

Full-SPARC sampling, with unresolved cases kept visible

All 175 galaxies and 3,391 radial measurements were included, allowing uncertainty in distance, disk inclination and stellar mass-to-light ratios. After targeted continuation of the initial 54 sampling failures, 157 of 175 meet the declared convergence diagnostics. 18 remain unresolved. The original passing cases and failed attempts are preserved.

The fit uses a fixed empirical acceleration law, the simple-nu comparator. These diagnostics assess sampling behaviour; they do not establish an adequate physical fit, an action-derived ITSM prediction, a new acceleration scale or preference over competing models. The next step is to diagnose the remaining cases and test assumptions and predictions.

Why the route has narrowed

Different calculations answer different physical questions.

Scoped failure

Alignment is not an automatic cure

The tested additional alignment restriction is not preserved by the original coupled dynamics. A whole-frame clock-restricted variant, F-tau, also fails a high-frequency gradient test on the homogeneous control. These findings reject specified routes, not every possible ITSM action.

Bounded control

A homogeneous linear estimate

R-tau admits a physical mixed-regularity linear bound on the old homogeneous benchmark. Its conservative constants do not imply precise finite-mode numerics, nonlinear stability or a usable interacting lifetime.

What the new mechanism screens tell us

13 October · separate declared controls, not a new accepted parent theory.

RM1 · Radial mass portal

A specific route fails

The assumed matter-mass portal provides a radial source, but not the required nonzero static coupling to the gapless phase mode. That mechanism is rejected for this particular purpose.

ST1 · Induced-scale control

A relation, not a selected number

A scalar–tensor control can provide a parameterized static source. Its kinetic ratio and matter-mass assignment remain inputs; a same-scale mass choice cancels the direct source.

ST2 · Heavy-field threshold

A calculable correction, with open inputs

A declared heavy field supplies a finite quantum correction. Renormalized boundary inputs and a viable physical background are still needed; the displayed vacuum potential has a stationarity obstruction.

What comes next?

The immediate calculation is the general-planar ordinary energy and both coupling blocks, followed by the actual higher-regularity remainder and nonlinear constraint domain. The completed state/history work can be reused while physical-domain and matching questions take priority.

In parallel, matching work can investigate the missing physical inputs without declaring a numerical prediction. If the action cannot earn the required domain, that calls for an explicit action-selection decision—not a silent change of model.

The wider programme

Galaxy rotation, lensing and clusters are eventual cross-scale tests. Expansion, structure growth, primordial abundances, gravitational waves and high-redshift galaxies need the appropriate background and perturbation equations first.

TOP-X4 remains a separate quantum-completion lane. Its bounded operator and state work does not yet supply the missing stress, gravity/ghost, parity, counterterm and physical-Hessian sectors. It has not replaced the current action route.
