A consistent first step on a changing background

A calculation with the background held fixed produced a nonzero constraint forcing for a declared class of small disturbances. Including the action-generated change in the background's constraint operator gives an equal and opposite term at first order. Both terms remain in the calculation; neither was removed to obtain the cancellation.

In plain language, these disturbances pass this first compatibility check when the changing background is included. The result applies to the declared amplitude-momentum disturbances under the report's assumptions—not arbitrary perturbations. The earlier frozen-background result remains valid: it answers a different question.

What it does not show: a solved physical evolution, stability, satisfaction of all higher constraint conditions or a controlled physical validity window. The numerical derivative checks are not integrated solutions or nonlinear error bounds. Existing energy and weak-source warnings remain.

The next step is to test the remaining constraint conditions and construct controlled short-time evolution from the previously established implicit initial data—or identify a precise incompatibility. This is progress within the existing R4C1 candidate action, not acceptance of a complete cosmology.

What the new mechanism screens tell us

13 October · separate declared controls, not a new accepted parent theory.

RM1 · Radial mass portal

A specific route fails

The assumed matter-mass portal provides a radial source, but not the required nonzero static coupling to the gapless phase mode. That mechanism is rejected for this particular purpose.

ST1 · Induced-scale control

A relation, not a selected number

A scalar–tensor control can provide a parameterized static source. Its kinetic ratio and matter-mass assignment remain inputs; a same-scale mass choice cancels the direct source.

ST2 · Heavy-field threshold

A calculable correction, with open inputs

A declared heavy field supplies a finite quantum correction. Renormalized boundary inputs and a viable physical background are still needed; the displayed vacuum potential has a stationarity obstruction.

What comes next?

The next bounded step keeps the R4C1 action unchanged: test the higher constraint conditions, including points where the equations change rank, and construct controlled short-time coupled background and perturbation evolution from the known implicit initial data. A sequence of frozen spectra cannot substitute for that construction. Physical energy, nonlinear error control, a usable interaction window and healthy general-relativity recovery remain necessary.

In parallel, matching work can investigate the missing physical inputs without declaring a numerical prediction. If the action cannot earn the required domain, that calls for an explicit action-selection decision—not a silent change of model.

The wider programme

Galaxy rotation, lensing and clusters are eventual cross-scale tests. Expansion, structure growth, primordial abundances, gravitational waves and high-redshift galaxies need the appropriate background and perturbation equations first.

TOP-X4 remains a separate quantum-completion lane. Its bounded operator and state work does not yet supply the missing stress, gravity/ghost, parity, counterterm and physical-Hessian sectors. It has not replaced the current action route.
