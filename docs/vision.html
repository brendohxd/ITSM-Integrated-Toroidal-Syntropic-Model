A connected picture.
A clear physical test.

ITSM starts with a proposal about what the vacuum is—and asks whether its consequences can explain gravity and cosmic evolution in one consistent framework.

The starting point

Not empty.
Not yet established.

The central hypothesis is that the observable vacuum is an active, finite-density condensate, called the plenum. Its density and phase can support excitations and circulation; its interactions with matter may have gravitational consequences.

ITSM combines that hypothesis with compact, periodic space and an observable subsystem that can exchange energy and entropy with a reservoir. These are proposed ingredients to investigate, not discoveries about the actual universe.

The ambition is not to make the gravitational effect of missing mass disappear: any proposed plenum response must carry a consistent stress-energy budget. Its behaviour in galaxies and in the early universe has to be calculated, rather than assuming that replacing particle haloes automatically satisfies cosmological matter and abundance constraints.

What the programme aims to explain

One framework must work across scales.

Galaxy dynamics & lensing

Determine whether the plenum can account for phenomena attributed to missing mass, while predicting both matter motion and the bending of light with the same parameters.

Expansion & structure

Calculate whether matter–plenum exchange and the vacuum's stress can produce a viable expansion history and the growth of cosmic structure.

Topology & thermodynamics

Find out whether periodic boundary conditions, circulation and open-system exchange produce distinct, measurable effects rather than only a descriptive analogy.

How the proposal can be tested

A research roadmap—not a list of observations already explained.

Motion and light

The same physical model must account for galaxy rotation, gravitational lensing and cluster dynamics, while recovering tested local gravity. Parameters cannot be retuned independently for each scale.

Expansion and structure

An action-derived history must connect expansion, structure growth, the cosmic microwave background and primordial element abundances. A fitted background or a standard-cosmology solver control is not a completed ITSM test.

Waves and the early universe

Derive wave propagation and any circulation-related spectrum, then test gravitational-wave and pulsar-timing data. Early-galaxy and black-hole proposals likewise need predictions and survey selection effects before comparison with observations.

A specific variant can fail through inconsistent or unstable dynamics, or through preregistered observational predictions incompatible with the data. Predictions, parameter choices and uncertainty models must be fixed before using target data to judge success. Read the full test programme.

What “syntropic” means here

The proposed observable system is open. Local organization can develop while energy and entropy are exchanged with an environment. The research must account for both sides of that exchange and test total conservation and entropy production.

This is not a claim of free energy, reversed time or an automatic violation of the second law. The current thermodynamics draft develops an accounting framework; a physical reservoir mechanism and a complete stability result remain open.

How ITSM relates to other models

General relativity supplies the gravitational baseline that any viable continuation must recover within its declared limit. ΛCDM supplies a comparison cosmology—not a fit that ITSM can claim as its own.

MOND-like acceleration laws and condensate analogues motivate useful questions and conditional controls. Neither a successful comparator nor a suggestive numerical relation establishes an ITSM derivation.

What would make the idea successful?

  • A specified action with consistent exchange, stable dynamics and a demonstrable physical domain.
  • A matter coupling and force normalization fixed by independently justified physics, with all remaining assumptions explicit.
  • Predictions that survive independent tests of galaxies, lensing, local gravity and cosmology without retuning between them.

Negative results count too: if a mechanism fails, the programme must narrow or abandon that mechanism. The present work has not established that ITSM replaces dark matter, resolves the Hubble tension or predicts a universal acceleration scale.
