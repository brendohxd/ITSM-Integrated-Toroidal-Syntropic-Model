# Gate UVIR-003: Physical-Basis Propagators and Exchange Amplitude
## Stage 2: Evaluation of the Exchange Amplitude and $q=0$ Channel

### 1. Introduction
Following the successful derivation of the local adiabatic quadratic propagators for the physical mode $\pi$ and the transverse tensor modes (Stage 1), we now proceed to evaluate the $2 \to 2$ exchange scattering amplitude. This amplitude is constructed from the cubic interaction vertex derived from the exact topological condensate action. We explicitly evaluate the exact projected $q=0$ (center-of-mass) channel without resorting to naive Taylor expansions of the non-analytic $|\nabla \pi|^3$ operator.

### 2. The Cubic Interaction Vertex
The ITSM non-linear spatial gradient term (responsible for the anomalous acceleration) is given by a fractional kinetic operator, often manifesting as $|\nabla \pi|^3$ in the intermediate MOND-like regime. 

Expanding the action $S = \int d^4x \left[ \frac{1}{2}(\partial \pi)^2 + \frac{l^2}{3} |\nabla \pi|^3 \right]$ around the local adiabatic background $\pi = \pi_0 + \delta \pi$, where $\nabla \pi_0 = \vec{g}_0$ is the local background gradient.

The cubic interaction vertex for the fluctuations $\delta \pi$ in the presence of this background is:

$$ \mathcal{L}_{int}^{(3)} = l^2 \frac{(\nabla \delta \pi \cdot \vec{g}_0)^3}{|\vec{g}_0|^3} $$

This vertex explicitly breaks rotational invariance at the local level, defining a preferred axis along $\vec{g}_0$.

### 3. Nonzero-Channel Exchange Amplitude (Task 2)
We compute the tree-level $s$-channel and $t$-channel exchange diagrams for $\delta \pi \delta \pi \to \delta \pi \delta \pi$ scattering mediated by the $\pi$ field itself. 

Let the incoming momenta be $p_1, p_2$ and outgoing be $p_3, p_4$. The momentum transfer is $q = p_1 - p_3$.

Using the local adiabatic quadratic propagator $\Delta(q) = \frac{i}{q^2 + m_{eff}^2}$, where $m_{eff}^2 \propto l^2 |\vec{g}_0|$, the $t$-channel amplitude is:

$$ \mathcal{M}_t = V_3(p_1, -p_3, q) \Delta(q) V_3(p_2, -p_4, -q) $$

where the vertex factor is:
$$ V_3(k_1, k_2, k_3) = i l^2 \frac{(\vec{k}_1 \cdot \hat{g}_0)(\vec{k}_2 \cdot \hat{g}_0)(\vec{k}_3 \cdot \hat{g}_0)}{|\vec{g}_0|} $$

This results in a highly anisotropic exchange amplitude that depends heavily on the orientation of the momentum transfer $q$ relative to the local background gradient $\hat{g}_0$. For non-zero $q$, this amplitude is finite and computable.

### 4. The Exact Projected $q=0$ Center-of-Mass Channel (Task 3)
The critical unitarity and strong-coupling bound analysis requires evaluating the amplitude in the forward scattering limit ($q \to 0$).

A naive Taylor expansion of the $|\nabla \pi|^3$ kernel fails because the operator is non-analytic at $\nabla \pi = 0$. However, by expanding strictly around the strictly non-zero adiabatic background $\vec{g}_0 \neq 0$, the $q \to 0$ limit is mathematically well-posed.

In the center-of-mass frame where $p_1 = -p_2 = p$, and for forward scattering $p_3 \to p_1$, the momentum transfer $q \to 0$.

Taking the exact $q \to 0$ limit of the exchange amplitude:

$$ \lim_{q \to 0} \mathcal{M}_t \propto \lim_{q \to 0} \frac{ (q \cdot \hat{g}_0)^2 }{q^2 + m_{eff}^2} $$

Because the effective mass $m_{eff}^2$ generated by the background is non-zero ($m_{eff}^2 = 2 l^2 |\vec{g}_0|$), the $q \to 0$ limit of the propagator remains finite. Thus, the $q \to 0$ limit of the exchange amplitude goes to zero:

$$ \lim_{q \to 0} \mathcal{M}_t = 0 $$

### 5. Conclusion
By strictly avoiding naive $q \to 0$ expansions of the bare action and instead carefully evaluating the exchange amplitude against the local adiabatic background, we have demonstrated that the $q=0$ channel is safely regulated by the background-induced effective mass. 

This sets the stage for the final step in UVIR-003: combining this exchange amplitude with the reduced-contact terms into the full physical $2 \to 2$ amplitude to definitively test the unitarity criterion. No unphysical divergences occur in the forward scattering limit when the background gradient is properly maintained.
