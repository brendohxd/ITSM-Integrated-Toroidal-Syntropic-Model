## 1. Thermogravity: acceleration is not sufficient (highest current relevance)

**Primary source:** Lee, Magueijo, van de Bruck & Di Valentino,
[arXiv:2609.29206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29206), submitted 24 September.
The abstract reports that universal non-conservation gives an SN+DESI BAO
fit about `Delta chi^2 = +51` worse than flat Lambda-CDM. The authors warn
that this universal case would change the observational dictionary. Their
CDM-only case instead reaches `Delta chi^2 = -4.85` and at most
`ln B = 2.77` for the background combinations considered; BBN/CMB ruler
calibration then increases the sound horizon and lowers the inferred `H0`.
These are *their model's* results, not an ITSM likelihood.

**ITSM use:** Specify both thermogravity variants as external background
controls for Tests 1, 15, 17–19. First derive ITSM's constituent source
vectors, `H(z)`, `q_dec(z)`, `r_s`, `r_d`, density histories, redshift and
luminosity-distance dictionary from the same action. Then compare the models
on identical uncalibrated SN+DESI BAO and calibrated BBN/CMB inputs, with
common data cuts and transparent prior/nuisance choices. Report `Delta chi^2`,
AIC/BIC and sampled evidence separately. A background fit cannot close Test 1
or certify stability, perturbations, local gravity or an `H0` solution.

**Near-term bounded task:** Freeze a comparison/input contract and identify
which required ITSM histories are actually available; do not run a purported
ITSM likelihood by inserting target histories.

## 2. H I–halo relation: observer-equivalent mass, not direct halo detection

**Primary source:** Korsaga et al.,
[arXiv:2609.27111](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27111), submitted 22 September.
The abstract reports `log10(M_HI/M_200) = -1.90`, about `0.36 dex` scatter,
and `7 <= log10(M_star/M_sun) <= 11.5` across SPARC, LITTLE THINGS, AMIGA and
GHASP. `M_200` is inferred through halo-dependent rotation-curve modelling.

**ITSM use:** Tests 2 and 8–10 should compare a fixed, independently derived
ITSM response across the samples and then process mock ITSM curves through
the *same* halo-inference pipeline to obtain observer-equivalent
`M_200^eff`. Assess normalization, scatter, isolation and high-mass behaviour
without a gas-dependent retuning. `a0 = c H0/(2 pi)` is **only a declared
conditional comparator** until Test 3 derives `C_chi` blindly; fixing it from
historical or observed values would not constitute an ITSM prediction.

## 3. J1351+0039: pressure-supported, nearly starless target

**Primary source:** Xu et al.,
[arXiv:2609.29852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29852), submitted 24 September.
The abstract reports `M_HI = 4.8e5 M_sun`, `M_star < 1.5e5 M_sun` and a
model-estimated `M_dyn = 1.3e8 M_sun`. The H I dynamics are dominated by
dispersion/pressure support rather than a clean rotation curve; the dynamical
mass estimate is not a direct particle-halo measurement.

**ITSM use:** Register as a Test-11 target. Before fitting, freeze the gas
geometry, temperature/turbulence, ionization, distance, external field and
equilibrium assumptions. Forward-model the line profile, size, kinematics
and (if measurable) lensing under a single conditional ITSM potential, and
compare to RELHIC/particle-halo controls. A SPARC-style circular-speed fit
alone would be the wrong observable.

## 4. Inelastic-dark-matter central deficits: competing mechanism

**Primary source:** Yang & Fan,
[arXiv:2609.29114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29114), submitted 24 September.
This is a theoretical representative-halo study, not a new observational
likelihood. It reports a non-monotonic velocity dependence and increasing
central deficits across four galaxy mass bins with weaker cluster-scale
behaviour.

**ITSM use:** Retain as a same-aperture, same-baryonic-control competitor for
Tests 8–10 and 13–14. A universal acceleration scale alone is not a
prediction of the observed mass trend. A quantitative test needs a fixed
ITSM response and forward-modelled galaxy/cluster populations; the paper's
representative-halo agreement cannot be scored as ITSM support or rejection.

## 5. Strong lenses: metric slip and geometric dictionary

**Primary source:** Zheng et al.,
[arXiv:2609.29377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29377), submitted 24 September.
Their abstract reports `gamma_PPN = 1.119 +/- 0.072` and
`Omega_k = 0.101 (+0.077/-0.076)`. It also explicitly uses an FLRW
distance-sum relation despite describing the analysis as model-independent.
The GR value is within about `1.7 sigma`, and flatness within about
`2 sigma`; neither central value is evidence for ITSM or toroidal topology.

**ITSM use:** Tests 7, 12 and 21 require metric potentials, null geodesics
and the actual ITSM distance-sum relation before refitting image positions,
kinematics, SN, BAO and chronometers. Keep a kiloparsec-scale effective
lensing/dynamical slip parameter distinct from the Solar-System PPN limit;
do not simply import the paper's FLRW-derived `Omega_k` as physical `T^3`
curvature. Marginalize lens-profile, mass-sheet and anisotropy degeneracies.

## 6. Two-rung TRGB: preserve local-field uncertainty

**Primary source:** Stiskalek, Desmond & Lavaux,
[arXiv:2609.29996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29996), submitted 24 September.
Across about 400 TRGB hosts and 80 local-field reconstructions, the abstract
reports `H0 = 68.4 +/- 5.9 km/s/Mpc` with **equal** realization weighting,
versus `72.3 +/- 2.6 km/s/Mpc` with **evidence** weighting. The latter is
dominated by one high-evidence realization and is explicitly described as
sensitive to finite reconstruction sampling.

**ITSM use:** Register as a separate Test-20 calibration branch and a future
Test-3 coefficient-audit input, not a means of selecting `C_chi`. Preserve
the full mixture and both weighting treatments. Recomputing the density and
peculiar-velocity fields under ITSM requires a derived growth history; until
then, either published posterior is a conditional external comparator. Do
not select the narrower high-`H0` branch to improve `a0` agreement.

## 7. QCD transfer: source-class-specific PTA control

**Primary source:** Franciolini, Pritchard & Tada,
[arXiv:2609.29078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29078), submitted 24 September.
The abstract reports up to about `55%` height changes, of either sign,
relative to radiation domination for **scalar-induced** gravitational-wave
spectra across the PTA band. Its NANOGrav 15-year fit shifts the inferred
primordial-spectrum peak parameters; this is not an ITSM feature detection.

**ITSM use:** VOR-001 and primordial-plenum/PTA work must include the
Standard-Model thermal history when predicting scalar-induced spectra, and
compare radiation-only to full-thermal controls. The tabulated transfer
functions are not automatically transferable to primary tensor or
topology-generated sources; derive their propagation/source equations
separately before applying any QCD correction. The proposed `1.45–1.88 nHz`
`T^3` feature remains unconfirmed.
