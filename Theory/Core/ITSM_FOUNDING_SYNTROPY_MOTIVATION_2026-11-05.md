## Founding intent to preserve

The initiating question was whether nature contains a physical organizing
influence that is a counterpart to the dispersive or disordering tendency the
operator associated with entropy. Polarity/duality motivated that question.
The proposed influence is called syntropy. This historical motivation belongs
to ITSM's research identity and must not be silently replaced by a narrower
interpretation as ordinary reversible heat exchange or bookkeeping alone.

The founding proposal does not impose equal opposing entropy changes,
exact cancellation, universal equilibrium, or a fixed balance between entropy
and syntropy. The thermodynamic consequences must be investigated, not
prescribed as part of the definition. An open-system entropy ledger is one
possible tool for that investigation; it is not the whole founding hypothesis.

## Scientific interpretation remains open

Entropy is a state/statistical quantity, not itself an energy or force. The
second law constrains entropy changes under its assumptions; it does not
forbid local ordering. The polarity examples are an originating heuristic,
not evidence that they share one physical mechanism or that every law must
have an equal opposite. Preserving the historical question does not establish
that it was overlooked by physics or that a new interaction has been detected.

The research task is to specify what an organizing influence acts on, its
equations and observables, and whether it gives consequences distinct from
known forces or from existing collective phenomena. Structure, coherence,
concentration, thermodynamic entropy and information entropy require separate
definitions; one must not be substituted for another without a physical map.
An entropy decrease, redistribution or increase accompanying new structure
must be a calculated result in a declared regime, not assumed cancellation.

Energy-momentum conservation and accounting for sector exchange are separate
from entropy balance. Neither conservation nor a nonzero exchange current
alone determines the sign of entropy production.

A scoped effective or phenomenological organizing-interaction hypothesis can
be investigated with declared assumptions and free parameters. Its microscopic
origin may remain open. Such work must be distinguished from an independently
derived mechanism or numerical prediction; missing microscopic derivations
must not automatically block unrelated, properly scoped results.
